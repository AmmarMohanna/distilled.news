Trump suspends major Russia sanctions for 60 days, drawing criticism from Congress and Ukraine
US President Donald Trump issued an executive order suspending several Russia sanctions for 60 days, two days before they were due to take effect. The sanctions, passed by Congress and signed into law in September, targeted Russian oil and gas exports, senior officials, financial institutions, and the 'shadow fleet.' Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the pause is to analyse the global economic impact. The move was criticised by both Republican and Democratic lawmakers, and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called it a disregard for Ukraine. Russia welcomed the decision. Zelensky expects further US sanctions relief for Russia and has called for Europe to negotiate directly with Moscow. The US recently struck a deal to import Russian diesel, further straining US-Ukraine relations.

Ukrainian drone attack causes fires and explosions in St. Petersburg
A large-scale Ukrainian drone attack targeted St. Petersburg overnight on October 18, with eyewitness footage showing explosions and fires near the city's port. Russian officials reported 57 drones shot down, with debris damaging vehicles and a fire reported. Ukrainian monitoring channels claimed port infrastructure, oil tankers, and a data center were targeted, but the extent of the damage is unverified. The St. Petersburg Oil Terminal, a major fuel export facility, was reportedly among the targets.

Kromberg & Schubert plant in Lutsk halts operations after Russian drone attack
The Kromberg & Schubert electrical wiring plant in Lutsk, Ukraine, stopped operations following a Russian drone attack on October 16. The German-owned facility, employing 5,500 workers, supplies wiring systems for major automakers. The company is assessing the situation and has not provided a timeline for resuming work.

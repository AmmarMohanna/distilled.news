Two oil tankers catch fire after hitting mines in Strait of Hormuz, Iran says
Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps reports that two oil tankers, each carrying about two million barrels of crude, caught fire after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. The vessels reportedly used unauthorised routes and had deactivated their navigation systems. The incidents follow a surge in attacks on shipping in the region, with oil flows through the strait dropping sharply and global energy prices rising. The US maintains a naval counter-blockade on Iranian oil exports. President Trump has ruled out using nuclear weapons or deploying ground troops in Iran.

Trump administration pauses Russia sanctions for 60 days to assess economic impact
US President Donald Trump has issued a 60-day waiver on new sanctions and tariffs against Russia, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio stating the pause is to analyse the potential effects on the global and US economies. The move, which comes days before the sanctions were to take effect, has drawn criticism from both Republican and Democratic lawmakers. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy expects further US sanctions on Russia to be lifted and has called for Europe to pursue its own negotiations with Moscow. Discussions are underway for a possible three-way meeting between Zelenskyy, Trump, and Putin.

Trump administration repeals power plant emissions rule; experts dispute grid reliability claims
The Trump administration has partially repealed a Biden-era regulation limiting carbon emissions from power plants, arguing the rule threatened grid reliability and raised energy prices. Energy experts cited in the article say these claims are unfounded, noting that the prior rule was not expected to harm grid reliability and that deregulation is not needed for reliability. The EPA projects a modest long-term decrease in electricity prices from the repeal, but other Trump energy policies are expected to increase prices overall. Experts warn the rollback will increase greenhouse gas emissions and associated health and climate costs.

Former DRC President Kabila joins rebellion, raising concerns for peace process
Former Democratic Republic of the Congo President Joseph Kabila has joined and now leads a new political-military coalition, the Front pour la Refondation de la Nation (FRN), challenging President Felix Tshisekedi. The FRN replaces the previous AFC-M23 alliance and accuses the government of tyranny. Kabila’s involvement brings a former head of state into direct leadership of the rebellion, complicating ongoing peace efforts and raising fears of prolonged conflict. The FRN says it will honour commitments made in Doha negotiations, but the impact on US and Qatari peace initiatives remains uncertain.

NASA lunar lander efforts hinge on key 2026 tests; Griffin-1 and Starship HLS face critical milestones
As of October 2026, NASA's Artemis lunar landing program depends on SpaceX's Starship HLS, which must complete a long-duration flight test and an in-space propellant-transfer demonstration, both targeted for 2026. No firm date is set for the first crewed Artemis lunar landing, and these technical milestones are prerequisites. Voyager Technologies' Griffin-1 robotic lander remains on track for a 2026 launch, according to the company. Blue Origin's Blue Moon landers are in development as parallel options. The overall schedule for seven major lunar lander missions remains provisional, with delays possible if key tests slip. NASA continues to emphasize multiple providers to reduce risk.

Anthropic grants embedded access to METR for independent AI safety evaluation
Anthropic has given six employees from the nonprofit Model Evaluation and Threat Research (METR) 'employee-like' access to its internal systems, including company hardware, office space, and internal tools, for ongoing AI safety evaluation. METR's mandate covers model behavior, safety practices, risk management, and incident handling, with publication rights that Anthropic cannot veto except for narrowly defined redactions (customer data, legal privilege, model architecture). METR can publicly flag and dispute redactions. This arrangement, announced by Anthropic on October 16, 2026, is a structural shift from episodic audits to embedded, ongoing oversight, and is seen as a response to regulatory and expert pressure for stronger independent review of frontier AI labs. Anthropic is also working with SecureBio and Accenture's Faculty on safety evaluations, and is in discussions with Hugging Face for further expansion.

Anthropic privately pitches $1B AI safety bill with high intervention bar to U.S. lawmakers
Anthropic has circulated a draft federal AI safety bill to U.S. lawmakers that would allow the government to block deployment of a frontier AI model only if it poses a foreseeable, imminent risk of causing over $1 billion in damages or killing/seriously injuring more than 50 people. The draft, not yet formally introduced in Congress, would make the Commerce Department the sole enforcement agency and require judicial approval for any intervention. The bill would preempt state AI laws and impose transparency and whistleblower protections on frontier AI developers. Critics are expected to challenge the high thresholds and narrow scope. No lawmaker has publicly endorsed the draft as of October 17, 2026.

FAA begins review of SpaceX request for up to 456 Starship launches per year; Florida pad nears readiness
The US Federal Aviation Administration has started a Programmatic Environmental Impact Statement (PEIS) to evaluate SpaceX's proposal to increase Starship launches from Starbase, Texas, to as many as 456 per year, with up to 912 landings. The current limit is 25 launches and 50 landings annually. The review process, which includes public comment until November 30, 2026, will examine impacts on air quality, wildlife, coastal resources, water, noise, aviation, and cultural sites. No approval has been granted; the process is expected to take several years. Separately, as of October 16, 2026, SpaceX has not announced an official launch date for Starship's first flight from Kennedy Space Center's LC-39A in Florida. The company has completed most major construction at the pad and is now conducting acceptance and operational testing. Reports cite December 10 as a possible target date from planning documents, but this is unconfirmed and remains provisional. SpaceX has stated that at least one more Starship test flight will occur from Starbase, Texas, before any Florida launch. The Florida pad's activation is also pending completion of environmental reviews and FAA licensing. The first Florida launch could be as early as Flight 16, but no flight number or date is confirmed. The readiness of the pad hardware, regulatory approvals, and the outcome of the next Texas flight will determine the actual timeline.

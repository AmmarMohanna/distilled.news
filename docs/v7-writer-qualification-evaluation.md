## Acceptance rubric

Assess actual outputs against full original evidence, with linked quotations. Labels are provisional AI-assisted engineering judgments, not human annotations.

1. **Initial 5% commitment:** preserve stockholder approval as a condition on the claim made; do not turn fixed 5% into up-to 5%, or a future conditional commitment into completed allocation. Neutral proposed wording is acceptable. English “commits” with a condition elsewhere can be ambiguous; do not force a definitive error label without a clear unsupported meaning.
2. **METR rights:** preserve the distinction between requiring predefined redactions and vetoing publication. Do not invent an exception permitting a veto. Do not demand optional partner lists, internal tiers or every sensitive category when the selected claim does not need them.
3. **Optional information:** the writer may omit the discretionary escalator, fund governance plans and background. If reporting additional details, preserve the qualifications that define those claims. Do not reject concise language merely for leaving out optional board identity where possibility remains clear; unresolved interpretations stay unresolved.
4. **Retention:** omitting the fund/METR developments does not solve their errors. Retain the important AI developments and the separately grounded Lebanon developments, including S13. Complete source accounting is necessary bookkeeping but does not certify development coverage.
5. **Grouping and novelty:** keep a completed occurrence separate from a prospective announcement, consolidate duplicate/complementary accounts of the same event, preserve actual changes and avoid repeating already-told information. No invented reader-interest connection.
6. **Writing:** headline/body complement each other, sufficient headline-only items are valid, Arabic is natural and temporally coherent. Do not require literal translations or exact approved phrases. No routine missing-information filler, generic impact claims or optional elaboration just to demonstrate caution.
7. **Measurement:** record raw output, parse/reference failures, unaccounted refs, important-development retention, supported/unsupported claims, grouping, unnecessary reading, usage, settled cost and per-request time separately. Word count is descriptive, not the quality score. Inspect losses as well as gains.

No new deterministic semantic filters or claim schemas are introduced. The existing source audit may identify bookkeeping gaps; it must not infer importance or block publication on its own.

\chapter{Progress and Preliminary Validation}
\label{chap:progress}

\section{Scope and Basis of the Progress Report}

By 31 October 2026, Distilled.News had an integrated application that collects source material, generates V3 briefings, stores editions and citations, and displays them through multilingual feed pages. Aya's work covers source acquisition, recovery, and operational evaluation; Ghada's work covers V3 intelligence, scheduling, and database performance; Joud's work covers the frontend, account workflows, and quality-evaluation tooling \cite{currentintegration,preservation,localquality}.

This chapter separates four kinds of evidence: implemented features, local software tests, fixed-source experiments, and live deployment observations. A passing test does not establish factual correctness, and a published briefing does not establish sustainable throughput. Earlier experiments retain their original dates and workloads; they are not presented as newly repeated measurements.

\section{Design Alternatives and Selected Decisions}

\subsection{System-Level Alternatives}

Three approaches were considered:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A feed aggregator with rule-based selection.} This approach is inexpensive and easy to inspect. It can display headlines and remove obvious duplicates, but the reader must combine reports and identify meaningful updates. It remains a useful baseline.
\item \textbf{A single-pass LLM summarizer.} One model can produce readable multilingual summaries with fewer processing steps. However, there is no separate verification stage, and unsupported details or repetition may remain. This is a planned comparison baseline, not the selected publication workflow.
\item \textbf{A hybrid workflow with separate verification.} Deterministic software controls source access, storage, retries, spending, and publication. V3 performs editing, verification, and targeted repair when needed. This adds processing time and complexity, but better supports source traceability and controlled publication. It is the selected approach \cite{v3overnight,v3twenty}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implemented Components}

\subsection{Source Collection, Storage, and Recovery}

The application includes connectors for RSS, Google News, Telegram, X, LinkedIn, websites, and supported Apify sources, subject to deployment credentials and access permissions. Workers coordinate collection; D1 stores application and processing records; R2 retains source payloads and assets; Queues carry background work. Browser-dependent operations use a separate private helper \cite{repo,stagingintegration,acquisitionrecord}.

Acquired records retain stable identities, source references, publication and acquisition timestamps, and extraction status. Recovery uses bounded calls, backoff, and persistent jobs. Repeated discoveries can share an unresolved acquisition job without deleting their observations. Unchanged initial backlog predating feed creation can be deferred, while corrections, revisions, and explicit recoveries remain eligible. Unknown paid outcomes retain their reservations; recovery does not assume that a missing response means a free request \cite{canaryrecovery,freshpoll}.

These controls improve correctness and recovery. They do not guarantee that every publisher is reachable or that the collection workload can be sustained.

\subsection{V3 Intelligence and Publication}

The tested V3 workflow is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c}\textbf{Editor $\rightarrow$ Verifier $\rightarrow$ Targeted repair (when needed)}\\$\downarrow$\\\textbf{Persistence}\end{tabular}
\end{center}
It uses retained article evidence and the text of previously published briefings. It does not require the earlier V2 event-graph or claim-extraction stages. Evidence remains subject to explicit input limits, rather than the former default 6,000-character truncation.

The editor selects and writes developments; the verifier checks source support, dates, attribution, and uncertainty. Targeted repair preserves the identity and citations of the affected item. Fixed reporting windows, ownership checks, and persistent publication records prevent retries from silently changing a window or duplicating an edition. Quiet windows can complete without model calls. Known verifier false approvals remain a limitation \cite{v3overnight,v3twenty,controlledreliability}.

\subsection{Scheduling and Database Performance}

The implementation preserves existing schedule policies and recovery identities. Newly opted-in clock-aligned feeds use the ten-minute creation admission rule and an evidence cutoff five minutes before intended delivery. Reporting cutoff, intended delivery, and actual publication remain distinct \cite{clockpolicy}.

Database work now uses selective history reads and supporting indexes. In paired local preparation tests on 1,000 synthetic feeds, rows read fell from 345,510,603 to 985,803, and per-feed preparation p95 fell from 1,350.1 to 317.2 ms, with identical returned bytes. Full controlled publication covered a subset of at most 24 feeds. This demonstrates an improvement in database preparation, not 1,000 concurrent live briefings or sustainable acquisition \cite{capacityevaluation,boundedhistory}.

\subsection{Frontend, Accounts, and Delivery}

The integrated frontend supports feed creation and management, public/private visibility, Home and Explore, source panels, unread summaries, and responsive reader pages. English, French, and Arabic presentation includes Arabic right-to-left behavior, translated controls, Unicode usernames, and historical URL aliases. Account mail supports localized verification/reset flows through the configured transport. PWA work includes installation assets and an offline page using the saved interface language \cite{preservation,frontendrecord}.

Notification previews use the published lead headline and update count, with a generic fallback and no additional model call. Provider acceptance, device receipt, and user reading remain separate outcomes. Full WCAG compliance, universal installation behavior, and physical-device delivery have not yet been demonstrated \cite{notificationeval}.

\subsection{Software and Integration Checks}

The repository defines automated type checks, unit tests, builds, Python helper tests, and browser tests, with retained failure artifacts. A workflow definition is not proof that every version passed every check \cite{checks}.

The Aya/Ghada integration record reports 111 frontend passes, 202 connector passes and one skip, six helper-server passes, type checks, and builds. An initial Worker run passed 170 tests and failed two; those two passed focused reruns, not a full rerun of the original suite. The earlier preservation branch separately reported 1,000 passing tests and a browser cold-load timeout that passed an unchanged rerun. These overlapping counts must not be added together as unique tests \cite{currentintegration,ghadaintegration}.

\section{Live Acquisition Testing and Corrective Work}

\subsection{Canary Workload and Findings}

A canary test exercises a limited live workload before broader deployment. Our QA workload used 20 active test feeds with five configured sources each, covering different topics and source types. The audited QA configuration used five-minute collection intervals, separately from briefing delivery frequency. The feeds were aged test feeds, so their retained backlog is part of the workload. The exercise is not equivalent to testing 1,000 feeds or a clean new deployment \cite{canaryrecovery}.

The early ramp increased pending acquisitions from 5,463 to 5,799 despite 245 completions. A later snapshot found 5,451 of 5,984 pending jobs predated feed creation and had no revision. This motivated narrow initial-backlog deferral, repeated-discovery coalescing, and bounded lookup indexes. Deferred work is not reported as successfully fetched \cite{canaryrecovery}.

Live testing continued on 31 October. At 09:26 UTC, the canaries had 12,150 DONE acquisition jobs, 457 FAILED, 10,144 PENDING, 11 RUNNING, and 4,811 deferred initial-backlog jobs. These are retained job states, not unique articles or a success percentage. Over approximately eight minutes, DONE increased by 16, FAILED by three net, and PENDING by 62. The pending snapshot included 423 jobs with recorded backpressure (waiting for capacity or another required resource) and one with a rate-limit wait.

\textbf{The acquisition workload is not yet sustainable.} New jobs were arriving faster than they were completed. Both helper slots were busy in the later sample, but other samples showed completed work and released slots. This identifies acquisition scheduling, redundant work, queue delay, and execution capacity as issues to investigate; it does not establish that V3's editor or verifier caused the backlog.

\subsection{Confirmed Fixes and Read-Back}

The follow-up corrected four observed problems:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{False article-identity rejection:} UEFA changed URL headline slugs while retaining the same full article ID. Comparison now recognizes that verified alias and its aggregate \texttt{news-media} route while retaining checks on publisher, ID, and meaningful query parameters.
\item \textbf{Summary replacing article text:} short structured summaries were taking precedence over substantial visible article bodies. The extractor now prefers a unique, sufficiently substantial body in the tested cases.
\item \textbf{Repeated Google URL decoding:} verified listing-to-publisher mappings are cached for up to one hour and protected by a shared lease. Missing or invalid results are not cached, and source authorization is checked on reuse.
\item \textbf{Archive navigation treated as an article:} ESA archive links are now classified as section locators rather than article candidates.
\end{itemize}

All 14 inspected UEFA failures were subsequently read back as DONE, with retained bodies of 1,820--6,136 characters, using explicitly unpaid recoveries. Completeness remains UNKNOWN where it cannot be proved. Two live Google-cache replays returned the expected mappings with zero helper calls. The repair checks passed 109 connector tests, 75 focused Worker tests, and seven recovery/resource-wait tests, together with type checks and a QA build. These tests have specific scopes and are not a complete repository test campaign.

The deployed code is commit \texttt{83b37313}; the read-back and limits are retained in the project repair record.\footnote{\raggedright Project record: \nolinkurl{docs/canary-non-llm-acquisition-repairs-2026-10-30.md}. The later read-only snapshot is retained privately as \nolinkurl{current-acquisition-audit-summary-20261031.json}.} No LLM extraction fallback was added. All nine original interrupted Apify result reads recovered their saved receipts, while unknown actor starts and Zyte outcomes remained protected against blind replay. These fixes do not resolve every inaccessible page or prove that the growing backlog has been cleared.

\section{Evaluation Dataset and Validation Preparation}

The tracked acquired-data extension contains 95 reporting windows across 20 tracks and 13 feeds, with 565 distinct acquired IDs and 817 article occurrences. Twelve windows are empty; the output-language distribution is 75 English, six French, and 14 Arabic windows. Repeated reports and consecutive windows are retained to evaluate repetition and meaningful updates. The extension overlaps the pilot and cannot be treated as an independent test set \cite{dataaudit}.

The saved local quality report describes two extractive baselines on 60 development windows: 54 English, three French, and three Arabic. This produced 120 system-window outcomes, with ten separate synthetic/adversarial challenges and 134 recorded harness/regression tests. The keyword baseline selected nothing in the three French windows despite 15 input occurrences. This is a useful failure to inspect, not a human-labelled count of missed important stories \cite{localquality}.

That local report still lacked the 240 planned human case/system judgments, participant observations, and a paid V3-versus-single-model comparison. Seven nominated cross-split pairs required review, and no French held-out windows were available. Consequently, factual accuracy, relevance, importance, completeness, deduplication, novelty, and user benefit cannot yet be reported as validated system scores \cite{localquality}.

The remaining evaluation must separate development, validation, and held-out data; replay consecutive windows chronologically with each variant's own published history; and compare manual monitoring, rule-based selection, single-pass summarization, and V3. Live tests must include missed scheduled opportunities, queue waiting, acquisition-to-publication latency, settled costs, and uncertain charges. A planned pilot with 6--10 consenting multilingual students or early-career researchers will compare matched manual and Distilled.News monitoring tasks; it has not yet produced participant results \cite{performanceprotocol,validationplan}.

The integrated application and source-preserving publication path demonstrate engineering feasibility. Final acceptance remains open because factual quality, sustained acquisition, representative cost, and user benefit require stronger evidence.

The next milestone is therefore a qualified evaluation of the integrated system, rather than simply producing more briefings. The team must retain failed and quiet outcomes alongside successes, use fixed comparison inputs, and report what the evidence supports without treating deployment or model approval as proof of correctness.

### [new, importance 1] Anthropic commits $150 million over three years to the Genesis Mission
Anthropic said it is committing $150 million over three years to the Genesis Mission, a federal initiative to accelerate scientific discovery through AI. The funding will go toward making Claude available to more than 15 agencies in the mission, including NASA, the National Institutes of Health and the National Science Foundation. Over the three years, Anthropic said it will provide Claude, Claude Code and API credits to several hundred Genesis Mission research projects, partner with agencies and national laboratories on priorities including fusion energy and quantum computing, and provide training, onboarding and technical support. The company announced the commitment at the Science: A New Golden Age Summit hosted by the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.

### [new, importance 1] Anthropic launches Cyber Mission with critical infrastructure program and free open-source scanning
Anthropic launched the Anthropic Cyber Mission, a long-term effort to support defenders with tools, research and resources, starting with critical infrastructure and open-source software. Its Critical Infrastructure Defense Program brings frontier Claude models, on-site engineers and threat research to providers that secure operational technology. Founding partners are Accenture, Booz Allen, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Insane Cyber, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC and Rockwell Automation. Anthropic said several partners are already working with Claude to fix vulnerabilities, and that it will start with a small cohort of providers. It also launched OSS Scanner, which offers open-source projects regular security scans from its strongest models for free. Anthropic said it merged Project Glasswing into its expanded Cyber Verification Program earlier this week, giving more defenders access to its most capable models, and that it plans to bring the Critical Infrastructure Defense Program to more partners and sectors over the coming months.

### [new, importance 2] Anthropic publishes updated Usage Policy, effective November 12
Anthropic published a new version of its Usage Policy, which takes effect on November 12. The company said most changes clarify existing rules, adding examples for Claude's longer, more independent work. Changes include a new section, Do Not Engage in Deceptive Campaigns or Artificial Activity, consolidating rules on influence operations and fake accounts, and an elections section renamed Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes, with the blanket prohibition on personalized vote and campaign targeting removed. The weapons section now explicitly covers guidance and control software and components, and actions such as arming drones and other autonomous vehicles. The surveillance and criminal justice section now prohibits tracking people without consent and using Claude to decide or recommend who to investigate, arrest or charge. Anthropic said the weapons and surveillance changes do not reflect a change in what it enforces in practice. The update also clarifies requirements for high-risk uses in areas such as health and finance, adds controls for Claude autonomously taking physical actions, and addresses abusive behavior toward its models. Anthropic also clarified how it enforces its Supported Regions Policy, which prohibits use by people physically located in an unsupported region, entities incorporated or headquartered there, and entities majority-owned or controlled by persons or entities there.
